The capstone and its defence

Five criteria, 100 points

20%

20%

10%

30%

Full marks, one example each: a card a colleague turns into a plan without asking anything; a plan whose deciding result is a number written before the data; the note that flips the decision first; a recommendation that names its kind (description, experiment, causal analysis, prediction or none) and why; a defence that changes the plan where the constraint bites and keeps it where it does not.

The result

Three parts make the result; the defence has its own floor

PartWeight
Supervised framing check, without AI20%
Framing card, plan and review notes, on the rubric50%
Live defence with an unseen change of constraint30%
To pass: 70% overall, and at least 60% on the live defence.

The rubric's first four criteria (70 points) make the 50% of the written work; its fifth (30 points) is the defence. A strong repository cannot carry a defence below 60%: the defence is where the decisions are shown to be the participant's.

The top note of your variant and its consequence

During the talk the sponsor changes one constraint. The ten-minute defence starts from it: what changes in your recommendation, and what does not?

The assessor plays Fahda. Deliver the change at about minute five, in her voice, in one sentence. Kinds of change that work: a frozen budget, a deadline brought forward, a dataset that becomes unavailable, a council member's new question. Use a different one for each participant.

Practise it now

A practice constraint change: what happens to your plan?

Fahda Al-BishrThe council has frozen hiring for this year. Whatever you recommend must work with the reviewers we have.
  • Does the decision still exist? Which options are left?
  • Does the kind of analysis change: from a causal question about staff to a description of where time is lost?
  • What stays the same in your plan, and why?

This one is in the repository (practice_constraint_change.md); the defence uses another. Give pairs five minutes: one plays Fahda, one answers in two minutes. The common weak answer restarts the plan from nothing; the strong one says exactly which line of the card and the plan moves.
